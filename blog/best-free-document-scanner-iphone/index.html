How to Scan a Document on iPhone for Free

You don't need to pay anything to scan documents on your iPhone. There are two free ways to do it, and which one you pick depends on how often you scan.

Option 1 — Apple Notes (no app to install). Your iPhone can already scan documents to PDF for free:

  1. Open the Notes app and start a new note.
  2. Tap the camera icon, then choose Scan Documents.
  3. Point your camera at the page — it auto-detects the edges and corrects the angle.
  4. Tap to capture, keep adding pages if needed, then tap Save.
  5. Share the note or export it as a PDF.

This is fine for the occasional one-off scan, but Notes mixes your scans in with everything else and has no folders or text search.

Option 2 — A dedicated free scanner app. If you scan regularly — receipts, contracts, lecture notes — a purpose-built app is faster and keeps everything organized:

  1. Open the scanner app (we use Scanory, a free iOS app for scanning documents).
  2. Point the camera at your document — auto edge-detection crops and straightens it.
  3. Scan multiple pages and they combine into a single PDF.
  4. Save it straight into a folder so you can actually find it later.

Either way, scanning a document to PDF on iPhone is completely free. The real question is whether the free app stays free — which is exactly what we tested below.

What to Look For in a Free iPhone Document Scanner

Before we compare apps, here's what actually matters:

  • No watermarks. A scanner that stamps its logo on your documents is useless for anything professional.
  • PDF export. You need to share and store scans as PDFs -- it's the universal standard.
  • Organization. Scanning 100 documents means nothing if you can't find the one you need.
  • Free means free. Not "free with limits" or "free trial." Actually free.
  • Privacy. Your documents contain sensitive information. Where does the app send your data?

Adobe Scan: Powerful, But the Free Tier Has Limits

Adobe Scan is a well-made app backed by a company that practically invented the PDF. The scanning quality is excellent, and the OCR (text recognition) is among the best in class.

The catch: The free tier limits OCR to 25 pages across 5 documents per month. Need more? That's $9.99/month for Adobe Acrobat Premium. For casual use, the free tier works. For students or professionals scanning regularly, those limits hit fast.

Privacy note: Adobe Scan processes documents in the cloud for OCR. If you're scanning sensitive documents, that's worth considering.

CamScanner: The Veteran With Baggage

CamScanner has been around for years and has a massive user base. The feature set is comprehensive -- scanning, OCR, collaboration, cloud storage.

The problems:

  • Watermarks on free scans. Every document you scan on the free tier gets a CamScanner watermark at the bottom. This makes free scans unusable for professional purposes.
  • Privacy history. In 2019, CamScanner was removed from the Google Play Store after a malicious module was found in the app. The issue was resolved, but trust is hard to rebuild.
  • Aggressive upselling. The free experience is designed to push you toward a $29.99-39.99/year subscription.

Apple Notes: Already on Your iPhone

Apple Notes has a built-in document scanner that's surprisingly decent. Open Notes, tap the camera icon, select "Scan Documents." It auto-detects edges, corrects perspective, and saves the scan to your note.

Where it falls short:

  • No dedicated folder system for scans. Your scans live inside notes, mixed with everything else.
  • No OCR. You can't search text within scanned documents.
  • Limited PDF management. Exporting scans as PDFs requires extra steps.
  • No dedicated scanning workflow. It's a note-taking app that happens to scan, not a scanner app.

For the occasional one-off scan, Apple Notes is fine. For anything more, you need a dedicated app. (If you're looking for more productivity tools that are actually free, we put together a roundup of 5 free iPhone apps worth checking out.)
