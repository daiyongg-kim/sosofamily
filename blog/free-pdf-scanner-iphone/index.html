Free PDF Scanner for iPhone — Scan to PDF With No Watermark (2026)

Scanning a page on an iPhone is easy. Getting a PDF out of it — one file, several pages, in the right order, small enough to email, without a watermark stamped across it — is where most free apps quietly stop.

That's the part this guide is about. Not "which scanner app is best" in general, but specifically: how to get a clean multi-page PDF off your iPhone without paying for it.

The short version

  1. Scan every page into one document before you export. Don't scan them one at a time and try to merge later.
  2. Export as PDF, not JPEG. A folder of images is not a document.
  3. Check the file size before you send it. A 12-page scan can easily come out at 30 MB, and plenty of mail servers reject anything over 25 MB.
  4. Check for a watermark. Some apps only add one on export, so you won't see it until the file is already in someone's inbox.

How to scan multiple pages into one PDF

The order matters more than people expect, because the free tier of most apps limits you at export time rather than at capture time.

  1. Open your scanner app and start a new document — not a new scan. The distinction is the whole trick: a document holds many pages, a scan holds one.
  2. Capture each page. Most apps auto-detect the edges; if the lighting is uneven, a flat surface and indirect light beats the flash almost every time.
  3. Reorder or delete pages before exporting. Fixing page order after export means redoing the PDF.
  4. Export once, as PDF. You should get a single file whose page count matches what you scanned.

With Scanory that flow is: new document → scan the pages → save. The pages stay together as one document, and exporting gives you one PDF. There's no page cap and no watermark, which is the specific thing that tends to bite on free tiers.

A few notes on that table, because the nuance is where the annoyance lives:

Apple Notes is genuinely capable and already on your phone. Notes → new note → camera icon → Scan Documents gives you a multi-page scan with no watermark, free, forever. What it doesn't give you is a document library: scans live inside whatever note you made, so finding a specific contract six months later means remembering which note it went into. If you scan two things a year, Notes is the right answer and you can stop reading here.

CamScanner's watermark is the classic free-tier trap — the scan looks clean in the app and the mark appears on the exported PDF. Worth knowing before you send a signed contract to a client.

Adobe Scan doesn't watermark, and its OCR is very good. The cap is on how much OCR you get before it asks for a subscription.

Keeping the file small enough to send

This is the step people skip, and then the email bounces.

A scan is a photograph. At full resolution, a colour page is several megabytes, so a dozen pages lands in the tens of megabytes. Three things help, in order of impact:

  • Scan in greyscale or black-and-white unless the colour carries meaning. A text document in greyscale is a fraction of the size and often more readable.
  • Don't scan at maximum resolution for text. Text stays legible far below the setting that makes a photo look good.
  • Send a link instead of an attachment when the file is genuinely large — iCloud, Drive, anything. It also avoids the recipient's mailbox quota.

Scan to PDF, then what

Two habits make the difference between having a PDF and being able to find it later:

Name it at export. "Scan_2026-09-09_14-32.pdf" tells you nothing in six months. "Lease — signed — 2026-09.pdf" tells you everything.

Put it in a folder immediately. Scanory keeps documents in folders you name, which is the piece Apple Notes doesn't offer. Whatever app you use, the filing has to happen at scan time — nobody goes back and organizes a scan pile later.

A word on privacy

Scanned documents are unusually sensitive: contracts, IDs, medical forms, bank letters. It's worth knowing whether your scanner uploads them.

Scanory processes and stores documents on the device. Apple Notes does too (subject to your iCloud settings). Cloud-first scanners upload by design — that's not automatically wrong, but it should be a decision you made rather than one you discovered.

Frequently asked questions

How do I scan multiple pages into one PDF on iPhone?

Start a new document rather than a new scan, capture every page into it, then export once as PDF. Merging separate one-page files afterwards is more work and more likely to end up in the wrong order.

Can I scan to PDF on iPhone without a subscription?

Yes. Apple Notes does it with no account and no cost. Scanory does it with folders and no page limit, also free. The apps that charge are charging for OCR, cloud sync, or watermark removal — not for the scan itself.

Why does my scanned PDF have a watermark?

Because the app added it on export. This is a free-tier limitation in some scanners, most notably CamScanner. Check the exported file, not the in-app preview — the mark often only appears on the export.

Why is my scanned PDF so large?

Because each page is a photo. Scan in greyscale, drop the resolution for text-only pages, and the file usually shrinks by most of its size.

Does scanning to PDF keep the text searchable?

Only if the app runs OCR. A plain scan is an image of text, which means you can read it but your phone can't search inside it. Adobe Scan's OCR is strong within its free allowance; for receipts specifically, ReceiptZero runs OCR entirely on-device and pulls out the vendor, date, and total.
