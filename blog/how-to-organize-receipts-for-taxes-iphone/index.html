How to Organize Receipts for Taxes on iPhone (2026 Tax Year)

Organize receipts for taxes on iPhone: scan, auto-fill vendor, date and total, sort into categories, and export a PDF or CSV. With 2026 tax-year dates.

Updated November 25, 2026 for the 2026 tax year (filed in 2027).

Here's the finish line: when you sit down to file, every receipt from the year is already a row with a vendor, a date, a total, and a category. One export gives your accountant a PDF or CSV, so there's no shoebox to dig through at filing time.

You can get there on an iPhone with ReceiptZero. It reads receipts with on-device OCR, so the vendor, date, and amount are filled in for you. It's free to download, and the free version has limits on exports that we'll spell out below. This post works backward from that finished state and ends with the 2026 tax-year dates to plan around.

Work Backward From the Export

Start with what the person receiving your records needs. An accountant doesn't want 300 photos. They want something they can drop into a return: a PDF to file, or a CSV to open in a spreadsheet or bookkeeping software. ReceiptZero produces both as tax-ready exports.

To get a clean export, each receipt needs four things:

  • A vendor. Who you paid.
  • A date. When you paid.
  • A total. How much.
  • A category. What it was for, such as meals, travel, or supplies.

A camera roll has none of those. A photo can't be added up, sorted, or turned into a CSV, so you'd still have to type every receipt into a spreadsheet yourself. A receipt that has been read at the moment you capture it is already most of a spreadsheet row; you just add the category.

Capture: Scan, Check, Categorize

Getting four fields onto every receipt takes about three steps per receipt.

  1. Scan the receipt. Open ReceiptZero, point the camera at it, and capture. On-device OCR reads the vendor, date, and amount.
  2. Check the fields. OCR can misread a smudged line, so glance at the vendor, date, and total before you save.
  3. Categorize it. On the triage screen, tag each receipt with a category; the dashboard shows what's still unclassified. Tag it while you still remember what it was for. Sorting as you go is what keeps the year organized instead of leaving it for the night before a deadline.

Do step 1 at the moment you pay. Scanning with the receipt in your hand beats trying to reconstruct a purchase months later. The app can't restore a receipt that has already faded, so scanning early is the habit that matters.

A year-round routine

  • Scan when you pay. Before the receipt goes into a pocket or glovebox.
  • Categorize within the week. The context is still fresh.
  • Export every quarter or so. Pick a custom date range on the export screen; a quick PDF or CSV means nothing piles up. If you're a Canadian self-employed filer making quarterly installments, those dates are March 15, June 15, September 15, and December 15 for the 2027 tax year.
  • Run a test export in January, not April. You'll see what the free version gives you and can decide about Pro with time to spare.

If you're mid-year already, don't try to catch up in one sitting. Scan the newest receipts first and work backwards.

What the Free Version Exports (and What Pro Adds)

Scanning and OCR let you build the whole year's records for free. The limits show up when you export, so it helps to know them before you're up against a deadline:

  • Free PDF export: every page carries a "Free Preview" watermark.
  • Free CSV export: includes only the first 10 receipts.
  • Pro: exports without the watermark and with every receipt in the CSV. Pro is an annual subscription or a one-time lifetime purchase.

So the free version is a good way to capture and organize. If you want a clean PDF to hand to someone, or a CSV with your whole year in it, that's what Pro is for. We'd rather you know that up front than find out at export time.

Deadlines and Retention for the 2026 Tax Year

Receipts do the most work in the weeks before filing. Here are the dates to plan around.

United States (IRS)

  • Tax Day for the 2026 tax year: April 15, 2027.
  • Form 1099-NEC for 2026 payments: due to recipients and the IRS by February 1, 2027, because January 31 falls on a Sunday (confirm on irs.gov). If clients send you a 1099-NEC, expect it around then.

We couldn't open every IRS page directly, so treat the US dates as "confirm on irs.gov" rather than gospel. Your tax preparer will have the final word.

How long to keep records. The IRS says to keep records until the period of limitations for that return runs out. In practice:

  • 3 years for most situations, or the later of 3 years from filing or 2 years from payment if you're claiming a refund.
  • 6 years if you leave out income that's more than 25% of the gross income shown on your return.
  • 7 years for losses from worthless securities or bad debt.
  • Indefinitely if you don't file or file a fraudulent return.
  • At least 4 years for employment tax records.

The IRS page we read says nothing about digital formats. With the US profile, ReceiptZero's PDF says "Recommended retention: 7 years (IRS)." That's a conservative default, not an IRS rule.

Canada (CRA)

  • Self-employed (you or your spouse or common-law partner runs a business), 2026 tax year: file by June 15, 2027, but any balance owing is still due April 30, 2027. (If the business's expenses are mainly tax shelter investments, the filing deadline is April 30.)
  • Most other individuals: April 30, 2027. The CRA's own page still showed the previous year's dates when we checked on November 25, 2026, so confirm the current date there.
  • Weekends and holidays: a deadline that falls on one moves to the next business day.
  • How long to keep records: at least six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to. When you export with the Canadian profile, the PDF includes the line "Retain for 6 years (CRA)." That's a reminder, not a substitute for the CRA's imaging conditions.

The CRA also says paper source documents imaged to its standards can be destroyed, with the image kept as the permanent record. The conditions include readable, commercial-quality images, a dated and signed logbook, and an indexing system so you can find each one. Our scanned receipts guide walks through that checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ReceiptZero free?

It's free to download, and you can scan, extract, and categorize receipts without paying. Exports have limits on the free version: PDFs carry a "Free Preview" watermark and CSVs include only the first 10 receipts. Pro removes the watermark and the 10-receipt CSV limit, and is an annual subscription or a lifetime purchase.

Do my receipts get uploaded anywhere?

OCR runs on your device. Backup goes to your own private iCloud and is on by default; you can turn it off in Settings.

Does it work for both US and Canadian taxes?

Yes. ReceiptZero supports US and Canadian (IRS and CRA) tax validation.

Can I give my accountant the records?

Yes. Export a PDF for your files or a CSV for a spreadsheet or bookkeeping software. On the free version, remember the watermark and the 10-receipt CSV limit.

What if the OCR misreads a receipt?

Review the vendor, date, total, and category before saving.
