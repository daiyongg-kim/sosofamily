Year-End Receipt Checklist for Freelancers: What to Do in December, January, and Before You File

A dated year-end receipt checklist for US and Canadian freelancers: what to do in December, January, and the weeks before filing, with 2026 tax-year deadlines.

Here is where you want to be by the time your accountant (or your tax software) asks for your records: every business receipt scanned, sorted into a category, checked for missing details, and exported as one PDF or CSV for the whole tax year, with a second copy saved somewhere else. Nothing in a shoebox, nothing "somewhere in my camera roll."

This checklist works backward from that finish line. It is split into three windows: December (gather and scan), early January (sort and fix), and the weeks before you file (export and back up). The steps use ReceiptZero on iPhone, but the order works with any system. It covers both the US and Canada. It is general information, not tax advice.

If you want to know whether a scan counts in place of the paper, or how long to keep records, we cover that separately in Are Scanned Receipts Valid for the IRS and CRA?. And if you want an all-year habit instead of a year-end sprint, see How to Organize Receipts for Taxes on iPhone. This post is only about the closing-the-year sequence.

The finish line: what a "done" year looks like

Before the steps, here is the end state in five lines:

  • Every receipt for the year is in one place, not split between email, photos, and paper.
  • No receipt is left as "unclassified."
  • Receipts that were flagged as missing required details (such as vendor, date, total, or category) have been fixed.
  • You have one export for the tax year (date range or fiscal year).
  • You have at least two copies of that export, plus the receipts themselves backed up.

The IRS describes good records as the ones that help you track deductible expenses, prepare your return, and support what you reported. It also says you can choose any recordkeeping system suited to your business, as long as it clearly shows your income and expenses. So the goal is a system that clearly shows the year, not a perfect one.

December: gather and scan

1. Make a list of where receipts hide. Paper in a wallet, bag, or car. Email receipts. Screenshots. Photos you took of a receipt and forgot. Subscription receipts from software and phone plans. You can't scan what you haven't found, so take a few minutes to list the places.

2. Scan the paper. In ReceiptZero, you can scan with the document camera or import photos from your Photos app. The app then reads the vendor, date, total, and currency on your phone, and it doesn't upload anything when you scan. Get the whole receipt in frame and flat, because a cropped total is an easy way to end up with a blank field.

3. Import the digital ones. Receipt screenshots and photos already in your Photos app can come in through the photo import. For an email receipt, take a screenshot first so it lands in Photos. Doing this in December, while you still remember what each charge was, is much easier than doing it in April.

4. Watch the year boundary. The app assigns each receipt to a tax year using the transaction date in your own time zone, so a receipt dated December 31 stays in that year. If your fiscal year doesn't start in January, you can change the start month. The default is a calendar year starting in January.

5. Don't throw anything away yet. The December job is capturing, not discarding. Whether and when paper can go is covered in the scanned-receipts post linked above.

Early January: sort and fix

This is the part most people skip, and it is the part that makes the export useful.

6. Clear the unsorted pile with triage. ReceiptZero has a triage flow that shows your unclassified receipts one at a time. You tap a category to classify, swipe to skip, mark a receipt personal, or archive it, and you can undo a mistake. The goal on screen is "Inbox Zero."

There are 15 categories: Advertising, Business Meals, Office Supplies, Phone & Internet, Travel, Fuel, Parking & Tolls, Professional Fees, Software & Subscriptions, Home Office, Equipment & Hardware, Training, Bank Fees, Insurance, and Other. If your accountant uses different headings, you can still start here and map them later in a spreadsheet.

Receipts are tracked in four states: Unclassified, Needs Review, Verified, and Archived. Your January target is that nothing is left in Unclassified.

7. Fix anything flagged as missing. The app checks each receipt for required details and marks the ones with gaps. The exact list depends on your country setting:

  • US (IRS) setting: total, date, vendor, and category.
  • Canada (CRA) setting: vendor, date, total, currency, and category.

A business-purpose note is not part of that check in the US setting. If your accountant wants one for meals or travel, add it yourself while the memory is fresh.

OCR is good but not perfect. Pick a handful of receipts and compare the totals and dates against the paper before you export.

8. Look at duplicates. If you scanned a receipt and also imported another copy of it, the app can flag a likely duplicate by comparing vendor, date, total, and currency. It only warns you and doesn't block saving, so you decide which one to keep.

9. Canada only: check your sales tax fields. In the CRA setting, if you've entered GST, HST, or both on a receipt, the app adds them up as an input tax credit figure. Whether you can claim it is a question for your accountant or the CRA. The US setting doesn't calculate anything like this.

10. Search for the stragglers. Use search by vendor or text, or the calendar view, to find days where you know you spent money for work but see no receipt. Receipts in more than one currency can be recorded as well.

Before you file: export and back up

Export and send

11. Export for the full tax year. Open the export screen, then choose either a start and end date or a Fiscal Year. You can filter by category and by status, and choose PDF or CSV. Archived receipts aren't included.

12. Know what the free version does. This is where we want to be upfront. ReceiptZero is free to download, but free exports have limits. The free PDF carries a "Free Preview" watermark on every page, and the free CSV includes only the first 10 receipts, with a note at the bottom saying "Showing 10 of N receipts." Removing those limits takes the Pro upgrade, which is an annual subscription or a lifetime purchase. It's worth running a test export in January, during the sort-and-fix step, rather than finding out the night before a deadline. On iPhone the app has no ads.

Two cases where ReceiptZero may not be the right pick: you're on Android and don't want ads, because the Android version is ad-supported; or you don't want to pay for a full CSV export, because the free CSV stops at 10 receipts.

13. Send the file to the person who needs it. The export is a regular PDF or CSV file, and you can send it to your accountant from the iPhone share sheet.

Back up and keep it private

14. Back up in two places. ReceiptZero's iCloud backup (your personal iCloud, not ours) is on by default, but it needs you to be signed in to iCloud, and a change to the setting takes effect after you restart the app. Then save the exported PDF and CSV somewhere separate from your phone, for example an encrypted drive. Face ID app lock is an optional setting if you want one.

15. Remember the receipt data stays yours. The App Store privacy label for ReceiptZero on iPhone says "Data Not Collected," meaning we don't collect data from the iPhone app. (The Android version is ad-supported.) On iPhone, your receipts live on your device and, if you've left iCloud backup on, in your own iCloud.

The dates to put in your calendar

These are the dates for the 2026 tax year, plus the first 2027 instalment date, that we could confirm when we wrote this on November 25, 2026. Check them on the IRS and CRA sites before you rely on them, because some come from secondary sources.

DateWhat happensApplies to
Feb 1, 2027Deadline for payers to send 1099-NEC and similar forms to recipientsUS
Feb 28, 2027 (last day of February)Deadline for payers to file T4 and T4A information returns with the CRACanada
Mar 15, 2027First 2027 instalment date, if the CRA asks you to pay instalments (later ones: Jun 15, Sep 15, Dec 15)Canada
Apr 15, 2027Federal return filing and payment deadlineUS
Apr 30, 2027Payment deadline for self-employed people who owe a balanceCanada
Jun 15, 2027Filing deadline for self-employed peopleCanada

The February dates belong to the people paying you, not to you. Still, they are why income paperwork starts arriving in late winter.

A few more points:

  • Canada, self-employed: only the filing date moves to June 15. Any balance owing is still due April 30.
  • US extensions: if you file for an extension, the filing deadline moves to October 15, 2027.
  • No 1099? Still report it. The IRS says you must report all taxable income even if you don't get a 1099-K or 1099-DA.
  • 1099-NEC is the form a business uses to report payments to people who aren't employees. Whether you receive one depends on the payer, so keep your own income records too.
  • Filing season start: the IRS hadn't announced when 2027 filing opens, so don't plan around a start date.
  • Use the forms as a cross-check. If a payer's total doesn't match your records, that's a good question to settle in February, not in April.

A one-page version to copy

December: list where receipts hide, scan paper, import digital, watch the year boundary.

Early January: triage to zero unclassified, fix flagged gaps, check duplicates, (Canada) check GST/HST, search for gaps, run a test export.

Before filing: export the full tax year, send it on, back up in two places, put the deadlines in your calendar.
